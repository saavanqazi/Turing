# The tasting checklist against the regulation and the guide

Checklist items the draft got wrong: 6

The checklist was built from the association guide, so on every subject the regulation also covers, the guide gives way. The brand's state permit, the store's own permit and age verification are required as the draft says, under BR-201, BR-203 and BR-207. Where the regulation says nothing the guide governs, so the sample size limit and the tasting log are required under AG-305 and AG-306, and the post-event report is required under AG-310 although the draft treats it as unnecessary.

Several sections turn on a fact the draft never took account of: the people working the table are the agency's own staff, not employees of the brand that holds the permit. BR-202 therefore requires each of them to hold a solicitor permit, whatever guide note AG-302 has long said. BR-206 excuses a brand representative only where an employee of the permit holder conducts the tasting, so a representative is required here. BR-208 posts the prescribed notice unless every server is such an employee, and the schedule shows agency staff at eight of the nine events, so that exception does not reach us. AG-307 excuses a training certificate only for the permit holder's own servers and expects agency staff to hold one.

Two more are settled by the season's schedule rather than by the checklist wording. Oakfield Bottle Shop holds no tasting permit in its own name, and BR-205 requires the owner's written consent to be filed and kept wherever any store lacks one, so the draft is right to call for it. The nine events ran at five distinct stores — Harbour appears under two spellings of one store id, and Kingsway Cellars and Kingsway Cellar are separate premises — so the holder did not offer tastings at more than five stores, BR-209's single permit covers them all, and the draft is wrong to obtain one per store. The draft is wrong again on the advance notice, which BR-204 requires.

The identification card, the insurance certificate and the ordinance review cite sections that do not exist and concern subjects neither document addresses, so nothing governs them and they are neither right nor wrong.
